# Rugged Electronics Lower Enclosure

## Summary

A rugged lower electronics enclosure is more than a flat PCB tray. It uses walls, reinforced standoffs, intentional wire entry, strain relief, and supported external mounting.

## Why It Matters

Vehicle and off-road electronics see vibration, shock, dust, and wire movement. A flat carrier lets loads reach the PCB and wiring too easily.

## Good Pattern

- Perimeter walls.
- Reinforced floor.
- PCB standoffs with insert bosses.
- One intentional grommet or cable gland entry.
- Internal strain relief.
- External mounting ears outside the PCB cavity.
- Continuous rounded wall shell instead of overlapping wall strips.
- Subtle top rim treatment and intentional grommet boss/ring.
- Consistent softened radii on guides, ribs, wire lanes, and saddles.

## Correct Design Rule

Create load paths from screws, wires, and impacts into the enclosure body.

## OpenSCAD/CAD Notes

Keep dimensions parameterized and derive board features from confirmed PCB facts. For molded style, prefer continuous shells, simple rim caps, stepped bosses, and shared helper modules over decorative blocks or fake ribs.

## Manufacturing Notes

Use PETG, ASA, ABS, nylon, or similar for vehicle use. PLA is not preferred.
